# 0.1 Basic creed — Day 3: the messengers
Date: 2026-11-28

## Belief in the messengers (pillar 4)
- Allah sent a messenger to every nation, calling to worship Him alone
  (al-Naḥl 16:36). Some are named to us, some are not (al-Nisāʾ 4:164).
- Believing in some messengers and rejecting others is disbelief; the
  believers make no distinction among them in belief (al-Nisāʾ 4:150–152).
- Twenty-five prophets are named in the Qurʾān (e.g. the list in
  al-Anʿām 6:83–86). The well-known figure of 124,000 prophets comes from a
  report in Musnad Aḥmad whose authenticity scholars dispute.
- Muḥammad ﷺ is the Seal of the Prophets: no prophet after him
  (al-Aḥzāb 33:40; Bukhārī 3535). He was sent to all people, while earlier
  prophets were sent to their own people (Bukhārī 335).
- Ulū al-ʿazm ("those of resolve", al-Aḥqāf 46:35): commonly identified as
  Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā and Muḥammad ﷺ (cf. al-Aḥzāb 33:7, al-Shūrā 42:13).

## Nabī and rasūl
- Every rasūl is a nabī; not every nabī is a rasūl.
- A common explanation: a rasūl is sent with a message to deliver
  (often a new law); a nabī receives revelation and may follow an earlier
  law. Scholars word the distinction differently — note the version your
  teacher uses.

## What the primers say about messengers
- Ashʿarī/Māturīdī primers list four necessary qualities:
  ṣidq (truthfulness), amānah (trustworthiness), tablīgh (conveying the
  whole message), faṭānah (intelligence). Their opposites are impossible.
  Ordinary human states (eating, sleeping, illness that does not lower
  their rank) are possible for them.
- ʿIṣmah: all Sunni schools agree prophets are protected from lying in what
  they convey and from major sins. Details beyond this (e.g. minor slips)
  are discussed at a later level.
- Muʿjizah (miracle): an extraordinary event that confirms a prophet's
  claim. The Qurʾān is the lasting miracle of Muḥammad ﷺ.

## Definitions
- rasūl (pl. rusul): messenger      - nabī (pl. anbiyāʾ): prophet
- khātam al-nabiyyīn: seal of the prophets
- ʿiṣmah: protection (from sin/error)   - muʿjizah: miracle

## Plain-words summary
- The CPU is fast; waiting for data is slow. Caches hide the wait when the
  next data you need is near data you just used (spatial locality) or was
  used recently (temporal locality).
- A working set that fits in a cache level runs at that level's speed; as
  the array grows past L1, L2, L3, time per element steps up.
- Random access defeats both the cache lines and the hardware prefetcher,
  so it shows the biggest jumps.

## Definitions
- working set: the data a loop touches repeatedly
- cache line: 64-byte unit moved between memory levels
- prefetcher: hardware that loads the next lines before you ask
- stride: distance in memory between consecutive accesses
